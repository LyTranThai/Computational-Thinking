## Exercise 2 - Team Contract 

A team contract sets clear expectations for how your team will work together, which helps you avoid conflicts and finish the project successfully. Discuss with your team and create a contract that every member agrees to. 

Search the internet for examples, then customize them to fit your team. Do not copy a template unchanged. The contract must reflect your team's own decisions. A typical team contract for a course project should include the following sections: 

- Team information: Team name, project title, and each member's full name, student ID, email, and preferred contact method. 

- Team goals and expectations: The project goal, the target grade or quality level, and how much effort each member commits to. 

- Roles and responsibilities: Each member's role (e.g., team leader, developer, tester, documentation, presenter), their strengths and weaknesses, and how roles may rotate or change. 

- Communication: Communication channels (e.g., Zalo, Discord, email), expected response time, and who is the main contact for the instructor. 

- Meetings: Meeting frequency, time, and place (in person or online), plus rules for agendas, taking meeting notes, arriving on time, and notifying the team of absences. 

- Work process and standards: Task assignment and tracking tools (e.g., Trello, GitHub Projects), internal deadlines, Git workflow (branching, commit messages, code review), and quality standards for code and documents. 

- Decision making: How the team decides (consensus or majority vote), and who has the final say when the team cannot agree. 

- Conflict resolution: The steps for handling disagreements, such as talking directly, then involving the team leader, then asking the instructor.
